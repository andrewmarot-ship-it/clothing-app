Grounds that let the clothes show

Most wardrobes are dark: black, navy, charcoal, denim, brown. The app's navy ground sits at almost the same lightness as all of them. Below, the same Outfit screen is shown in four palettes with the same 13 pieces. Under each phone, every piece that sits within 0.15 of the ground in OKLab colour distance is circled as hard to see.

  • The ground is as dark as the clothesThe navy background has a lightness of 0.21. Black pieces are at 0.21 too, and navy, charcoal and brown sit between 0.30 and 0.35.
  • Pieces off the centre are dimmedUnselected pieces are drawn at 72% opacity, which mixes them further into the background.
  • Dark shadows on a dark groundThe drop shadows under each piece are black, so on navy they blur the edges instead of lifting the piece.
  • A tinted ground skews colourThe ground is slightly blue, so cream and camel look warmer than they are. That's a problem for an app that judges colour.

My pick: Linen

Linen fixes the clothes people own most of, and it misses only white and cream. A hairline outline fixes those for a few lines of CSS. Linen is also close to the light surface people photograph their pieces on, so cut-outs look the way they did on the table. If keeping the dark look matters most, choose Lightbox instead.

Whichever palette wins, I'd also make these changes:

  • Stop dimming pieces that aren't selected. Size already shows which one is selected.
  • Give every piece a hairline outline in the palette's rim colour.
  • Keep the ground neutral, without a hue, so the tone matching isn't working against it.
